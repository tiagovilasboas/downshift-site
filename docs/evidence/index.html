Downshift by Tiago Vilas Boas · https://github.com/tiagovilasboas/downshift

Five kinds of "saving" appear around this project, and they are not the same number. This page separates them, quotes each figure from the file that holds it (checked against the current file on 2026-10-17), and says which rung the project stands on today. Rule of reading: a figure on a lower rung never stands in for a higher one.

Rung What it is Status today
A Theoretical saving from catalog list prices Arithmetic, always available
B Saving estimated by telemetry (normalised units, catalog-priced tokens) Available on Claude Code; an estimate per decision
C Saving measured in controlled benchmarks Partial: quality measured, cost not
D Saving demonstrated on real tasks, routed versus control One measurement, one operator, 2026-10-15
E Cost per task completed with acceptable quality Defined and computed by report; no window has unlocked it yet

A. Theoretical saving (list prices)

Source: internal/catalog/catalog.json (version 2, updated 2026-10-16), USD per 1M tokens.

Model Input Output Cache read Cache write
claude-haiku-4-5 (small, the native haiku alias) 1.00 5.00 0.10 1.25
claude-sonnet-5-5 (mid) 2.00 10.00 0.10 2.50
claude-opus-5-5 (frontier) 4.00 20.00 0.20 5.00

A Haiku child costs 75% less than Opus and 50% less than Sonnet on uncached input and output tokens. On cache reads the gap to Opus is 50% and the gap to Sonnet is zero. A subagent's traffic is mostly cached context (the one transcript checked in beta-exit.md had 18 input, 311 output and 82,900 cache tokens), so "75%" is the ceiling of one token class, not the saving on a child. Rung A also assumes the same tokens on both models and a task that passes on the cheaper one; both assumptions are what rungs C to E test.

B. Saving estimated by telemetry

Source: internal/telemetry/telemetry.go (Aggregate), export.go, stats-export.md.

  • Normalised units (norm_baseline, norm_routed): every decision is 1.0 baseline unit; an applied downshift is charged 1 - estimated_savings (a list-price ratio), an applied upshift upshift_cost_ratio, everything else 1.0. They are counts, not money: no tokens are involved and no price is applied.
  • Current telemetry output: stats reports observed token counts, coverage and dimensionless normalized units; report compares routed and control tokens per completed task; schema 3 export contains token and coverage fields with no USD keys. Older event logs can still contain legacy dollar fields, but current commands do not expose dollar amounts.
  • Provenance. billing-gap-audit.md (2026-10-09, 7 days): total 186, usage_records 15, usage_linked 5, actual_cost_usd 15.432977 and real_saved_usd 0.424866 from the then-current export, plus baseline_no_route 0; these are historical schema-2 fields, not current output. Native tokens and model matched the record on 5 of 5 linked children. That is provenance for token counts, not a bill. P3.4 needs 50 linked records and stands at 5 in that audit.
  • Tokens rerouted, and why "tokens saved" is not a claim. Routing does not claim to reduce tokens. The observed token counts show what the child used; dimensionless normalized units in stats are not dollars, and current output does not price those counts. "Tokens rerouted" counts the tokens of usage records linked to an applied, non-refuted rewrite, by harness and requested-to-ran model pair, split into moved to a cheaper model and moved to a pricier one. It is rung B evidence: counts are observed (Claude Code only today, because it is the only harness that records child tokens), normalized units are dimensionless, and the baseline stays a counterfactual. Older descriptions of dollar figures refer to pre-schema-3 output, not current commands. It carries these limits: a refuted rewrite, an unknown requested model or a decision with no usage record contributes nothing. Downshift does not claim fewer tokens: no run without routing exists to subtract from, a cheaper model can need more tokens or a retry, and on a plan with no per-token bill (Claude Code Pro, Max, Team; Cursor plans) what was saved is not tokens or dollars but headroom against a quota, which no hook sees.
  • "Tokens saved" in the live views means saved from the requested model (owner decision 2026-10-19). The session tree, session --panel (savvy-progress), the statusline, watch, dsmon, the child-end line and the stats headline show the share of child tokens that ran on a cheaper model than the one the harness asked for: the "cheaper" bucket above over every priced child (control, held, kept and refuted children count in the total, never as saved), per agent, per agent type and per session (telemetry.TokenSavings). Work tokens (input+output) and cache tokens (read+write) are counted apart, because cache reads are most of a child's tokens and carry the smallest price gap: Sonnet to Haiku saves nothing on them, so one share would overstate the value. These views carry no dollar; downshift report compares routed and control groups in tokens per completed task. The plan lens also states the comparison in tokens. The share is a count of where tokens ran, not a reduction in tokens and not a dollar saving. downshift try quotes no price or percentage for one decision: it says whether its tokens would run on a cheaper or a pricier model than requested, and only when the hook would make that rewrite. Per harness, what is observed and what blocks the rest: value-per-harness.md.
  • Known biases. baseline_source: inferred (Claude Code settings guess) is counted apart; a refuted rewrite saves nothing; an unknown requested model gives zero saving by construction; a decision with no usage record gives units only. The headline percentage this produces (19.3% in one operator's log, per subagent-routing-ab-2026-10-15.md) is an estimate per decision at list price, not a measurement.

E. Cost per task completed with acceptable quality

Not measured today. report compares routed and control tokens per completed task, reports rework, and prints no USD. It does not assess task quality. The token check is a point percentage with no interval; no window has met its 20-task-per-group threshold, and comparison with provider-dashboard billing remains open under P3.7.

downshift report compares routed and control token counts per completed task, along with rework rate and a point token check with no interval. It prints no USD in text or JSON. A completed task has a completed child end plus token usage; reworked attempts are joined to the completed task. It needs 20 completed tasks per group (MinReportTasks); at the default DOWNSHIFT_CONTROL_SHARE=0.10 that is roughly 200 completed tasks. Neither text nor JSON output prints a dollar figure.

What is missing to produce it: enough completed tasks in one log (the pilot at share 0.5); a captured PostToolUseFailure payload, without which rework undercounts (GAP-CHILD-FAILURE); and a quality judgment, because "acceptable quality" is not in the log: completed means the child returned, not that its work was right. downshift feedback <id> failed|retry is the manual backstop. The 2026-10-15 A/B measured cost per run with an acceptance check, which is close to E for those tasks but is not the report definition and had no rework term.

Three saving scenarios and what each must prove

The objective is one sentence: minimise cost per task completed correctly, with safety and developer experience preserved. It is never "pick the cheapest". The guardrails in internal/core/selfcheck.go (Check) encode that: R1 holds an unconfident downshift, R2 any downshift of a COMPLEX task, R5 any downshift of a task that touches a risk category (the risk floor, Guards.RiskFloor), R8 an unconfident upshift, R7 an unconfident frontier pick with no known current model, R9 the same for mid. Only a deterministic lift (deterministicLift: risk floor, risk abstention, graph escalation, an opted-in NB upshift, an operator tier floor) may move a task up. Doubt never spends money, in either direction (TestCostTruthTable, TestCostUnconfidentUpshiftNeverRewritesAbove in internal/core/cost_safety_invariants_test.go). A scenario below counts as proven only when its metric is measured with those guardrails on; a saving bought by removing them is a different product.

Where Downshift adds value beyond native opusplan and gateways

Three things sit near Downshift. Claude Code's native opusplan (claude-code.md, section 1) is a main-session policy: Opus while plan mode is on, Sonnet once the plan is approved; it makes no per-spawn decision, and a subagent inherits whatever the session runs. A gateway (LiteLLM, Portkey and the like, when-to-use.md) owns transport, keys, retries and fallbacks and sees each request after the harness has named a model. "Shown by" names a test or an evidence file; a claim with neither is marked unproven.

What Downshift does not do, by design (when-to-use.md, companions.md): it is not a gateway and terminates no HTTP chat or completions traffic; it holds no provider keys; it proxies no request and adds no markup; it never installs, launches, wraps or configures a companion (downshift context enable headroom writes one field in its own file; an earlier init call was removed in #71); it does not rewrite tool results (its compressor observes); it stores no prompt, task text or path. The only network call on the hook path is the optional, at-most-daily refresh of public capability scores, off with DOWNSHIFT_SCORES=off. A companion's saving is never counted as routing: doctor reports the companion so a before/after is not attributed to Downshift alone.

Where the saving can come from

Nothing in this section is measured. It is the arithmetic of the ceiling, to read before comparing any percentage above with another tool's.

Routing has a structural ceiling. It acts only on subagent spawns, only on the share of those a guardrail lets through, and only by the price gap between two models. The saving on total spend is at most:

subagent share of spend  x  price gap  x  routable share

Worked example, hypothetical, not an observation: if subagents are 30% of a session's spend, the price gap on the tokens that move is 50% (Sonnet to Haiku on uncached work tokens; zero on cache reads), and 35% of spawns are routable (b5 applied 19 of 56, b6 3 of 34), the ceiling is 0.30 x 0.50 x 0.35, about 5%. With a routable share of 10%, as in b6, it falls under 2%. This is why b5 shows 12% on a task set built from delegation while natural use is estimated near 3%: the first inflates the subagent share and the routable share, the second does not. The three factors are the quantities worth logging, not their product.

Output cutting acts elsewhere and multiplies. A tool that shrinks command and tool output (a compression proxy such as Headroom, an output wrapper such as downshift exec, or a third-party output-cutting tool) reduces the tokens the main session reads and then re-reads from cache on every later turn: the part of spend routing cannot touch. The two levers act on different tokens, so their savings compose roughly as (1 - r)(1 - o), not as a sum. Hypothetical: a 5% routing saving on top of a 20% output saving takes spend to 0.95 x 0.80, a 24% total saving. The composition breaks if cutting changes behaviour (more retries, more turns), which is part of what the arms of benchmark-output-cutting.md test.

Status. None of this is measured. No run has combined routing with output cutting; the 12% (rung D) and 3% (rung B estimate) are the only routing figures; no output-cutting figure from Downshift or its companions has been reproduced here. The protocol and its pre-registered cut criterion are in benchmark-output-cutting.md.

Why catalog prices are not the invoice

Catalog prices are public list prices from a dated snapshot. An invoice differs by plan (Pro, Max and Team have no per-token line at all), negotiated or batch discounts, cache pricing the catalog may lack (then cached tokens are billed at the input rate, an upper bound), provider-side retries and the parent session's own tokens, which no hook sees. The only invoice-grade comparison is the billing comparison template, still unfilled; see pilot.md for the protocol that feeds it.

Why fewer tokens can cost more

A cheaper child that fails is paid for and then paid for again on the retry, usually on a larger model, plus the parent's turns to notice and re-delegate. The outcome suite shows the risk size: the small tier passes 27 of 40 tasks where the frontier passes 40. That is why the proposed primary metric is acceptable completed tasks including rework; current report compares token counts per completed task and reports rework, but does not compute dollars or judge quality. It adds redone attempts to the completing child, and why an unconfident router that keeps the requested model (most spawns in b2 and b6) is the intended behaviour rather than a missed saving. A token count or per-decision share describes token placement; it does not establish task quality or invoice cost.
